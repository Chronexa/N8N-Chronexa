AI automation agencies build the software that does repeated work for you: reading documents, answering routine email, moving data between tools, drafting reports, checking work against your rules. The label is crowded. A design studio that added a ChatGPT workflow last year and a machine learning lab with fifteen years of research both call themselves AI automation agencies, and they are not interchangeable.

This guide helps you sort them. It covers the four types of agency, the questions that separate the ones who deliver from the ones who present, a shortlist by type, and how the pricing usually works. We are an agency ourselves, so we say where we fit and where we do not.

## The short answer

An AI automation agency is a firm that designs and builds automations and AI agents for other companies, usually on top of the software those companies already use. A good one starts by looking at how your work actually flows, builds one thing that works, and measures it before proposing more.

If you only read one section, read the questions below. They work on any agency, including us.

## The four types of AI automation agency

**Marketing and creative agencies that use AI.** They use off-the-shelf tools to produce content and campaigns faster. Fine for marketing output. Not the right pick if you need systems wired into your CRM, accounting software or documents.

**Automation specialists.** Smaller firms that build workflows with tools such as n8n, Make or Zapier, now increasingly with AI steps that read, classify and draft. They suit companies that want a defined process automated on existing software, quickly. This is where Chronexa sits.

**AI and machine learning engineering firms.** They build custom models, data pipelines and AI-powered products. The right choice when AI is part of what you sell or when you need a model trained on specialist data. Often more than a back-office automation needs.

**Platform vendors with services.** Companies that sell an AI platform and offer implementation help. Useful if you are committed to the platform. Check how portable the work is if you later leave it.

## Seven questions to ask any AI automation agency

1. **Who will build my system, and can I meet them before I sign?** The person who sells the work is often not the person who does it.
2. **What will I have in my hands after the first week?** A plan is not a result. Ask for something that runs on your tools.
3. **How do you rank what to automate?** The right answer is by return and risk, not by what looks impressive.
4. **Who owns the code, the prompts and the accounts?** If the work runs on the agency's accounts, leaving is painful.
5. **Where does my data go, and which models touch it?** Ask for this in writing, especially for client or financial data.
6. **Where does a person review the AI's output?** Anything that reaches a client or a regulator should have a named reviewer.
7. **What would make you tell me not to automate this?** An agency that cannot name a case where it would say no will build anything.

## Red flags

- A fixed quote before anyone has looked at your tools.
- Case studies with no numbers, or numbers with no method behind them.
- Everything built on the agency's own platform account, with no export.
- No answer on what happens when the automation fails at 2am.
- Pressure to buy a long retainer before a first result.

## How the four types compare

| Type | Best for | Typical engagement | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing agency using AI | Content and campaign output | Monthly retainer | Little integration with your core systems |
| Automation specialist | Defined processes on your existing tools | Fixed-scope builds, then support | Small team, so check capacity |
| AI and ML engineering firm | Custom models and AI products | Multi-month projects | Cost and scope beyond a back-office need |
| Platform vendor with services | Teams committed to one platform | Licence plus implementation | Lock-in to the platform |

## How AI automation agency pricing usually works

Agencies charge in three ways: by the hour, by a fixed project fee, or on a monthly retainer. Hourly pricing is hard to compare and puts the risk of debugging on you. Fixed fees against a written scope are easier to judge, and retainers make sense once there is a system to run and extend.

Whatever the model, ask for the price of the first useful result, not just the total programme. Our [guide to what AI automation costs](/ai-automation-cost) lists the factors that move the number.

## Choosing an agency in the UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

The same questions apply, with a few regional additions.

- **Ask for technical depth, not just marketing.** Request a demo of a live workflow. If an agency cannot show you the tools it builds with, it may be a reseller.
- **Check data residency.** If you are in healthcare (DHA or DOH) or finance (DIFC or ADGM), ask where your data is processed. A good agency can offer self-hosted or in-region cloud options.
- **Think about per-task pricing.** Some cloud tools charge per task, which can grow quickly at volume. Ask how costs behave as you scale.
- **Insist on a fixed-price first scope.** A defined first build makes agencies comparable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does an AI automation agency do?

It builds automations and AI agents that take over repeated work: reading documents, handling routine email, syncing data between tools, drafting reports and checking work against your rules. A good agency also tells you what not to automate.

### How much does an AI automation agency cost?

It depends on scope. Agencies charge hourly, by fixed project fee or by monthly retainer. Ask for a written price for the first useful result so you can compare agencies like for like. We start with a free audit and a free first workflow, then price each further build against its return.

### How long does it take to see results?

A single well-defined workflow can be running within days to a few weeks. Larger systems should be delivered in increments, so you see working results regularly instead of at the end of a long project.

### Should we hire an agency or build this in-house?

In-house gives you control and suits companies with engineers who can maintain the systems long term. An agency is faster to start and brings experience from other builds. Many companies start with an agency and bring the maintenance in-house later, which is why ownership of the code and accounts matters.

### How is an AI automation agency different from an AI consultant?

A consultant mainly advises on what to do. An agency builds it. Some firms do both. See our page on [hiring an AI consultant](/ai-consultant) if you want advice first.

### How do I check an agency's claims?

Ask for named references or detailed case studies with the method behind each number, talk to the people who would build your system, and ask for a small paid or free first piece of work before committing to a larger one.
