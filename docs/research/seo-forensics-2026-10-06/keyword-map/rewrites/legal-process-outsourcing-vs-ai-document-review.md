Legal process outsourcing, usually shortened to LPO, is when a law firm or in-house legal team hands defined legal work to an outside provider. The most common tasks are document review, contract drafting and review, legal research, due diligence support and e-discovery processing. Providers are often based in lower-cost countries, which is where much of the saving comes from.

It is a mature industry and it works. It is also the only lever many firms know they have when work outgrows the associates available. This guide explains what LPO is, how it is priced, where it falls short, and what changes when a private AI system does the first pass instead.

## What legal process outsourcing covers

Firms and legal departments typically outsource:

- **Document review**, including first-level review for litigation and regulatory matters, coding documents against criteria set by the supervising lawyers.
- **Contract work**, such as drafting from templates, abstracting key terms and reviewing large volumes of standard agreements.
- **Due diligence support**, where many documents in a data room need reading and summarising.
- **Legal research and drafting support**, handled by junior legal staff at the provider.
- **E-discovery processing and support**, including processing, hosting and review workflows.
- **Compliance and regulatory monitoring**, tracking changes and logging what matters.

The provider supplies the people and the process. The client keeps responsibility for the legal judgement, so a supervising lawyer reviews and signs off the output.

## How LPO is priced

LPO providers usually use one of four models.

1. **By the hour.** You pay for reviewer time. Flexible, but the total depends on how long the work takes.
2. **By the document or page.** A unit rate for each item reviewed. Easy to budget when the volume is known.
3. **A dedicated team.** A fixed monthly fee for a team assigned to you. Suits steady volumes.
4. **A fixed project fee.** A single price for a defined matter, which works when the scope is clear.

Rates vary widely with location, task complexity and the seniority of the reviewers, so ask providers for a written quote against a sample of your own documents. What they all share is that cost follows volume. Twice the documents next year means roughly twice the bill, because the provider is selling labour.

## Benefits and risks of LPO

**Benefits.** Lower cost per hour than local staff, quick capacity for a surge, round-the-clock turnaround across time zones, and no need to hire and train for a temporary peak.

**Risks.** Confidential and privileged documents leave your systems and sit on the provider's infrastructure, handled by people who are not your employees. Quality depends on the provider's training and quality control. Turnaround depends on their staffing and time zone. Most engagement letters and data handling policies allow for outsourcing, so it is usually not a compliance breach in itself. It is a dependency, and it becomes your problem when something goes wrong on the provider's side.

## What changes with a private AI review system

The alternative is not another vendor. It is a system that reads the same documents the outsourced reviewer would have read, applies first-pass criteria, extracts the clauses and risk flags that matter, and does it all inside your own environment on your own document management system. Nothing is uploaded to a third party, because there is no third party in this part of the workflow.

The system does not replace review. It changes what people spend review time on. Instead of every document being read at the same pace by an associate or an outsourced reviewer, the system reads them all first, flags what actually needs judgement, and a person reviews that smaller, prioritised set. Unambiguous items, such as a standard confidentiality clause or a routine schedule entry, get through without using review hours they never needed.

## When outsourcing still wins

It would be wrong to claim AI closes every gap an LPO fills. If a firm needs extra reviewer-hours on the ground for a fixed period, for example a large litigation matter with a hard deadline, outsourcing is built for that and software is not a substitute for temporary headcount. If volume is small and stable, a custom system may never pay for itself.

The calculation changes upstream of that decision. A firm that triages its document volume through a private system first and sends only the ambiguous, high-risk or judgement-heavy portion to outside reviewers is sending a smaller batch either way. The outsourcing bill shrinks because the volume needing a human shrinks, not because the rate was negotiated down. For e-discovery, the same logic applies to the earliest stage: a first-pass classification layer inside your environment narrows what goes downstream, which is where most e-discovery spend accumulates.

The honest trade-off is timing. Outsourcing starts working the week you sign. A private system takes weeks to build and tune before it handles real volume reliably.

## How to choose an LPO provider

If outsourcing is the right call, ask the provider about:

- Where documents are stored and processed, and who can access them.
- Reviewer qualifications and training for your matter type.
- Quality control, including second-level review and error rates measured on a sample of your documents.
- Confidentiality terms and how privilege is protected.
- How they handle surge capacity and what happens to turnaround time.
- What you get at the end: coded output, audit logs, and the format of both.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is legal process outsourcing?

Legal process outsourcing (LPO) is the practice of hiring an outside provider, often in another country, to carry out defined legal tasks such as document review, contract work, legal research and e-discovery support, under the supervision of the client's lawyers.

### What services do LPO companies provide?

Typically document review, contract drafting and abstraction, due diligence support, legal research, e-discovery processing and compliance monitoring. Some also provide paralegal and administrative support.

### How much does legal process outsourcing cost?

It depends on the task, location and provider. Pricing models include hourly rates, per-document rates, dedicated team fees and fixed project fees. Ask for a written quote based on a sample of your own documents so you can compare providers fairly.

### Is legal process outsourcing safe for confidential documents?

It can be, with the right contractual and technical controls, but the documents do leave your systems. Check where data is hosted, who has access, how privilege is protected and what your own engagement letters and regulators require.

### Can AI replace legal process outsourcing?

For recurring, high-volume review, an AI system inside your environment can do the first pass and reduce how much needs a human at all. It does not provide temporary headcount for a deadline. Many firms use both.

### Can this replace our e-discovery vendor?

Not entirely. It changes what reaches the vendor. A first-pass classification and extraction layer in your environment narrows the document universe before it goes downstream, which reduces volume and cost without ending the vendor relationship.

### Does an AI review system work with the platforms we already use?

It runs alongside your existing document management and review platforms. Results can write back to systems such as iManage or NetDocuments in the format your team already works in.
